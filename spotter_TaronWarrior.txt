1. One big thing for me is getting comfortable with GitHub again, especially how repositories store your files, how commits track your changes over time, and how pushing and pulling moves work between

2. Ive learned how two people work together through shared repos, where you might make a branch, make changes, and then create a pull request so your teammate can review before merging.
3. Building the EC2 portion taught me how servers work in practice. Connecting through SSH and cleaning up my volumes and elastic IPs to help prevent leftover charges in the console.
4. COBOL introduced me to thinking in terms of return codes, like 0, 4, 8, and 12, and what each number means as far as troubleshooting is concerned. Also showing how environment setup problems could 
happen in a work setting.
 5. Learning and familiarizing myself with the whole workflow, from doing multiple EC2 runs to taking screenshots and uploading the evidence to GitHub, basically teaches the full loop of 
set up work, document and clean up. Together those pieces build confidence not just in commands, but in the habits of software collaboration.
